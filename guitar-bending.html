Tip

Guitar bending basics

On guitar, a bend raises the pitch: you push or pull the string across the fret, which tightens it. The hard part isn't strength, it's landing exactly on the target note. Most players stop short and sound flat, especially on whole-step bends.

  • Hear the target first. For a full bend, fret two frets higher and listen, then bend until you match it. The Target button does the same thing.
  • Use three fingers. Bend with your ring finger and line your middle and index fingers up behind it on the same string.
  • Turn the wrist. Hook your thumb over the neck and rotate your wrist like turning a doorknob, instead of pushing with the fingertip.
  • Pick a direction. Push the G, B and high e strings toward the ceiling; pull the low E, A and D toward the floor.
  • Read the tab. 7b9 or 7 full means fret 7, bent up a whole step to the pitch of fret 9. "½" is a half step, "¼" a slight curl, and r means release.

Light strings (9s or 10s on electric) make big bends much easier. On acoustic guitars, half steps and quarter-tone curls are the norm.
